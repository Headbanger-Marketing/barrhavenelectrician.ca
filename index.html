Before work begins

Work backwards from the date the power must be ready.

Plans can differ from what gets built. In a recently built home with builder provisions, rough-ins, spare conduit and panel labels may reflect the original plan rather than what was finished. Photograph what is visible and compare it with the handover drawings.

Whatever the home, list the date each piece of equipment arrives or each person moves in, then count back through the notification, any permit and the contractor's schedule. Ask which steps cannot start until another is finished.

What papers should I gather before a closing or move-in date?

Collect the builder's drawings, the panel directory, any inspection report, earlier ESA paperwork and product sheets for equipment being delivered. Warranty documents belong in the pile too, because they may say who should touch which parts of the installation.

Can a delivery date drive which charger circuit I choose?

The date should shape the schedule, not the design. Ask the contractor to assess the property supply and the proposed load first, then say which steps depend on one another, so a late step is spotted early.

Which quote suits a job that must finish by a set date?

Line up what each written scope includes before looking at the dates it names. Check that every quote names the same equipment, the notification and the restoration of finished surfaces, and that none treats the deadline as a reason to skip an assessment.

Who has to approve changes if a closing date has not passed yet?

Until ownership transfers, the seller or the builder may control access and any alterations. Ask in writing who can authorize a visit, and do not let a contractor begin anything before that person agrees.

Does sending the form create a contract for my deadline?

Treat the form as an enquiry for review; it creates no contract, and your dates bind no one. Plan with the site, which neither schedules visits nor sets prices, and does no electrical work.
