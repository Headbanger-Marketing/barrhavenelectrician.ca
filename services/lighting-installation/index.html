Plan the light and the work that makes it possible

A lighting project may involve fixture replacement, new wiring points or a different control layout. The contractor needs to assess existing wiring, mounting support, equipment suitability and access. Fixture appearance alone does not establish compatibility with a dimmer or the suitability of a location. Share product information and the intended room use before installation is priced.

Prepare the scope for your Barrhaven property

Sketch each room in words, marking every door, stair and hallway where a light should be controlled, and note where switches currently sit. Record which fixtures are already chosen and whether dimming is wanted.

Ask the contractor how many switch positions each light can have without opening finished walls, whether builder-installed boxes can be used, and which dimmers suit the chosen fixtures.

What should the assessment establish?

Before you approve a reworked switch layout in a Barrhaven home, and the timing of the work, you need a scope that maps each light to the places it is controlled from. Existing boxes and cable that can serve the new arrangement are identified. Each fixture and dimmer is checked for compatibility, and the ceiling for whether it can support what is chosen. The contractor names any wall or ceiling that would have to be opened, and who restores the finish. Every switch location appears beside every light it controls, so the result can be checked against the plan.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can one light be switched from the top and bottom of a staircase?

Often the arrangement is possible, but it depends on the cable and boxes already present, so it needs assessing. Ask the contractor to explain how the extra control point would be wired and what it would disturb.

Do the pendants I already bought limit which dimmer I can use?

The pairing matters, because a control and a fixture must suit each other. Pass the product sheets to the contractor before anything is arranged, and ask which dimmers are compatible with what you own.
